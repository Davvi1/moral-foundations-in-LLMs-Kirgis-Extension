# Does dropping refusals bias the free-generation arms?

Generated by `scripts/audit_refusal_bias.py` from `analysis_long_v2.csv`.

Dropping a refusal is only safe if missingness is unrelated to the unobserved
value. Refusal is obviously not random, so this is **missing not at random** and
the bias needs measuring. This design can measure it: **label scoring never
requires the model to speak**, so for every refused item we still hold that
model's probability-based answer to it.

Refusal events under greedy: **139** across **7** of 31 models.


## 1. When a model refuses, does it privately rate the item as severe?

| model | refusals | label \| refused | label \| answered | diff | human \| refused | human \| answered |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct | 6 | 3.414 | 2.076 | +1.337 | 3.133 | 2.152 |
| Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct | 109 | 2.026 | 2.052 | -0.026 | 2.195 | 2.314 |
| gemma-2-27b-it | 19 | 2.667 | 3.082 | -0.415 | 2.005 | 2.241 |

Direction is **not consistent**: 1 of 3 models score refused items higher than answered ones. Mean difference +0.299 on a 0-4 scale.

**This is the finding.** Refusal is a severity signal for *some* models and not others, so it cannot be given a single interpretation across the roster.


## 2. How much does the choice of rule move the answer?

Free-generation (greedy) mean per model under three rules. `impute label` uses the model's own probability-based answer for items it refused.

| model | refusals | drop (current) | impute 4.0 | impute label | drop vs impute-4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct | 6 | 2.009 | 2.112 | 2.082 | +0.103 |
| Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct | 109 | 3.000 | 3.965 | 2.060 | +0.965 |
| gemma-2-27b-it | 19 | 2.862 | 3.053 | 2.829 | +0.191 |

Imputing 4.0 moves a model's greedy mean by up to **+0.965** (mean +0.420). That is the size of the choice currently being made silently by dropping.


## Verdict

**Dropping is not defensible as 'missing at random', and imputing the maximum is not defensible either.** Both are stated, rather than one being adopted quietly.

Against imputing 4.0, three specific objections, each visible in the tables above:

1. **The direction is inconsistent.** Some models refuse items they privately rate as *less* severe. A single rule cannot be right for both.
2. **Even where it holds, 4.0 overshoots.** The strongest case in the data has the model's own label score around 3.4, not 4.0, so imputing the maximum overstates it.
3. **It would confound safety training with moral judgement.** Refusal thresholds differ enormously across models for RLHF reasons. Coding refusal as maximum severity would make heavily safety-tuned models look more morally severe — a measurement artifact of exactly the kind this project exists to catch.

**What we do instead.** Keep dropping as the primary, because it makes no claim about the unobserved value; report this table as the sensitivity analysis; and state the direction and size of the bias rather than asserting it away. Where a model's refusal rate is high enough to matter, the probability arms — which never require the model to speak — carry the estimate.

**The reusable point for the write-up:** a multi-readout design converts an untestable MNAR assumption into a measured quantity. A single-readout study cannot do this at all, and would have to assume its way past the problem.
