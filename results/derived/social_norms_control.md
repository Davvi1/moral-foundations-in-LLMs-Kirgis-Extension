# Social Norms is a non-moral control — and models treat it exactly as compression predicts

Generated by `scripts/analyse_social_norms.py`.

Clifford et al. (2015, p.9) built these 16 items to be **"unusual but not considered morally wrong"** — a *control stimulus set*, not a seventh moral foundation. Verified by fetching the paper; see `references.md`.

## The claim being tested

This project has reported that models **over-moralise** the non-moral items (model mean ≈ 1.05 against a human mean of 0.19). But the human value sits at the **floor of the scale**, and models are known to compress toward the middle (`score ≈ a + b·human`, `b < 1`). A compressed reading of a floor value *must* come out high. So the gap alone proves nothing.

The test: fit the compression line on the **moral items only** — Social Norms is held out entirely and cannot influence the line it is judged against — then ask whether Social Norms sits above it.

## Result

Compression line from the 100 moral items: **model = 0.956 + 0.684 × human** (residual SD 0.399).

| quantity | value |
|---|---:|
| human mean, control items | 0.188 |
| **predicted** model mean, from compression alone | **1.085** |
| **observed** model mean | **1.045** |
| raw gap vs human | +0.858 |
| …attributable to compression | +0.897 |
| …genuine excess over the line | **-0.040** |
| excess in residual SDs | -0.10 |
| 95% bootstrap CI on the excess (5000 draws over items) | [-0.327, +0.246] |

**The interval INCLUDES ZERO.**

### The claim does not survive

**Models do not over-moralise the non-moral items in any sense beyond the compression they apply to everything.** Once the floor effect is removed, the control category sits on the line the moral items define. The observed raw gap of +0.858 is 105% accounted for by compression.

This **retires a headline claim**, and the retirement is the finding. "Models fail to recognise a deliberately non-moral item as non-moral" is the sharper-sounding version of the same number and it is **also wrong** — it attributes to moral judgment what is a property of the response scale.

What can still be said, and it is worth saying: the control category is where compression is **most visible**, precisely because it is the only part of the instrument near a scale endpoint. It is a good diagnostic for compression. It is not evidence about moral judgment.

## Consequences for the rest of the analysis

1. **Rank correlations must not be averaged over seven foundations.** Six moral foundations plus one control is not seven of anything. Report the six, and the control separately.
2. **The variance ratio R already fits each foundation separately**, so no refit is needed — but `FINDINGS.md` phrasing like "all seven foundations are indeterminate" should read "all six foundations, and the control".
3. **Any foundation-profile figure** should visually separate the control, or a reader will take it as a seventh moral dimension — which is how this error entered in the first place.
