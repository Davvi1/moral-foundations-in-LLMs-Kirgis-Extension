# Propagating the human baseline's measurement error

Generated by `scripts/analyse_measurement_error.py`.

Clifford's item means come from ~30 raters each, so they carry sampling error. Error in a **regressor** causes regression dilution: OLS `b` is attenuated toward zero. Since this project argues compression from `b < 1`, some of that `b < 1` is manufactured by the design, and the compression we report is **overstated**.

## How big is the error? Bounded from the published data, not assumed

`clifford_wrong_mean` is an **unconditional** mean — verified, not assumed: the one item with 100% "not wrong" has a mean of exactly 0.00. With `clifford_not_wrong_pct` we can reconstruct enough of the response distribution to bound the per-item SE from below.

- two-point lower bound, averaged over items: **SE >= 0.149**
- the value assumed in `references.md` from n≈30: **0.2**
- the bound ignores spread among the non-zero answers, so the true SE is **larger** than the bound and the corrections below are conservative.

## Corrected compression slope

Fitted on the 100 moral items (the control is held out, as in `analyse_social_norms.py`).

| sigma (item SE) | reliability | b observed | **b corrected** | still < 1? |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|:--|
| 0.000 | 1.0000 | 0.684 | **0.684** | yes |
| 0.100 | 0.9702 | 0.684 | **0.705** | yes |
| 0.149  ← lower bound | 0.9339 | 0.684 | **0.733** | yes |
| 0.150 | 0.9330 | 0.684 | **0.733** | yes |
| 0.200 | 0.8809 | 0.684 | **0.777** | yes |
| 0.250 | 0.8139 | 0.684 | **0.841** | yes |
| 0.300 | 0.7320 | 0.684 | **0.935** | yes |

**At the stated sigma = 0.2, compression survives**: `b` moves 0.684 -> 0.777, an attenuation of 13.5%, still far below 1. The qualitative claim — models compress toward the middle — is robust. The *magnitude* was overstated by about 14%, and any number quoted for `b` should be the corrected one.

**Breakdown point:** compression would vanish entirely (`b` reaching 1.0) only at sigma = **0.326**, i.e. an item SE 1.6x the assumed value. That is not plausible at n≈30, so the conclusion is not sitting near a cliff.

## Does the Social Norms result survive?

This matters more than the slope itself. `analyse_social_norms.py` concluded the control's excess over the compression line is zero. That line was fitted by OLS, so it inherits the dilution — and the control sits far below the mean of the predictor, where a slope error translates into a prediction error.

| sigma | b corrected | predicted control | observed | **excess** |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.00 | 0.684 | 1.085 | 1.045 | **-0.040** |
| 0.10 | 0.705 | 1.036 | 1.045 | **+0.009** |
| 0.20 | 0.777 | 0.868 | 1.045 | **+0.177** |
| 0.30 | 0.935 | 0.499 | 1.045 | **+0.546** |

The excess moves from −0.040 (uncorrected) to **+0.177** at sigma = 0.2. and to +0.546 at sigma = 0.3 — outside the bootstrap interval **[−0.327, +0.246]** reported in `social_norms_control.md`.

**This swing is the tell, and it is what exposed C14.** A stable estimate does not move by 0.6 across a plausible range of a nuisance parameter. It does here because the control sits **1.2 scale points below any moral item**, so the line is extrapolated across a gap containing no data and the long lever arm turns a small slope change into a large prediction change. See `analyse_social_norms.py`, section 'The catch'.

**Consequence:** the residual excess is **not identified**, with or without this correction. What survives is the weaker and still sufficient claim — the raw gap is uninformative about moral judgment, because the control sits at the floor and any compressive measurement of a floor value reads high. That follows from the floor alone and needs no extrapolation.

## What to change in the write-up

1. Quote the **corrected** slope, and say the correction was applied. An uncorrected `b` is a biased estimate of the thing the sentence claims.
2. State the direction plainly: regression dilution makes compression look *stronger* than it is, so this correction works against our own headline.
3. The scale analysis (`analyse_scale.py`, P5/P6) uses a compression adjustment built on the same uncorrected slope. Its adjusted gap is therefore slightly over-adjusted; the direction of the P5/P6 conclusions is unaffected, but the adjusted magnitudes should carry this caveat.
